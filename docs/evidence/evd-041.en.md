# EVD-041 · Observed fixed-runtime handler cost and stack use

[한국어](evd-041.md) · [Measurement design](../fixed-cost.en.md) · [#90](https://github.com/Romanof123/JNOX/issues/90)

All 24 initial wrapper invocations failed before QEMU started because the capture directory already existed. Their individual logs and statuses are archived. After correction, the first three actual cold boots of each of eight FIFO/RR configurations passed, with accepted UART and a stopped PC inside idle. No guest failed. Measurements used QEMU 6.2.0 on one virtual Cortex-A72.

The outer interval covers sampled Rust handler work, including timer service and optional trace publication. The inner interval covers the complete policy event. Entries give inclusive minimum–maximum counter ticks; each group spans three boots. Trace-off and trace-on measurements use distinct images.

All 12 instrumented boots reported 62.5 MHz (16 ns/tick). Calibration used one consecutive ordered read pair per boot and was never subtracted. Vector entry, assembly saves and restores, ERET and handler prologue/epilogue are outside the interval. Post-endpoint journal writes may perturb later decisions. Cost-off cells supply behavior and linked footprint only. Their differences cannot quantify timing overhead or rank the configurations.

Depths were identical across the three boots of each instrumented cell. They are observed lower bounds on deepest stack use and include the initial 832-byte frame. Restored bytes, coincidental paint values and untouched stack-pointer reservations can mask greater depth. Slot 3 retained zero throughout the final snapshots. Five 16 KiB stacks reserve 81,920 bytes in every image; instrumentation adds a 7,208-byte journal. A separate 16 KiB boot stack lies outside the paint measurement. These observed ranges establish no WCET, product timing/stack threshold, physical-board performance or safety qualification.
