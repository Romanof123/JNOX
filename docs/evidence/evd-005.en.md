# EVD-005 · Preemptive earliest-deadline-first dispatch

[한국어](evd-005.md) · English

**Passed on one emulated Cortex-A72.** LOW arrived first and had higher static priority. A physical timer released URGENT with an earlier absolute deadline. The interrupt returned into URGENT’s private stack. When URGENT finished, LOW resumed at precisely the instruction the timer interrupted. [Public acceptance contract](../m1-edf.en.md) · [Source candidate](https://github.com/Romanof123/JNOX/commit/2d5b5ffbda9b411c2c600f08808aae5f23b3646c)

| Observation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| First launch, 2026-11-24 | A fresh verifier recorded a passing seven-line, 101-byte UART transcript before inspecting the exception log, kernel source or linked instructions. The runner exited 0 after 0.1 s of silence. |
| Timer and context | The physical timer IRQ interrupted LOW at `0x402012bc`. Its exception return entered URGENT at `0x4020134c`. URGENT executed `SVC #1` at `0x40201388`, returning directly to `0x402012bc`. |
| Competing policies | LOW had priority 3, URGENT priority 2. LOW was requeued first. Both were eligible. The kernel derived absolute deadlines from guest counter reads with offsets of 60 s for LOW and 1 s for URGENT, and checked that URGENT’s deadline was earlier. Its EDF selector applied that order to the live ready queue. |
| Observer controls | Five host replays with malformed transcripts were rejected. An exact transcript with CRLF endings passed. The controls reused the observer without another guest. |

The frozen `edf-bench` release ELF SHA-256 is `1363c7fe8b0e354be13ce40a52eb2b07f2b7adc9e7da0efcbf664e1c619ac4f5`. It ran once on QEMU 6.2.0, GICv2, TCG, 1 CPU and 128 MiB.
