# EVD-018 · Priority inheritance, timeout and nested ceilings

[한국어](evd-018.md) · [Acceptance contract](../m1-mutex.en.md) · [Change #49](https://github.com/Romanof123/JNOX/issues/49)

On its first cold boot, the independent verifier reconstructed all 34 scheduling decisions on one emulated Cortex-A72. When HIGH blocked, LOW inherited priority 9 and ran ahead of MEDIUM. LOW’s unlock transferred ownership to HIGH and restored its base priority. Eight decisions covered this case.

In the 14-decision chain, HIGH’s donation passed through LOW to BASE. A real generic timer IRQ interrupted BASE at `0x402012fc` and expired HIGH’s wait. Both owners fell from 9 to 2 before the next choice. HIGH resumed at its saved acquire call. MEDIUM ran after HIGH exited. In the 12-decision ceiling case, LOW reached priorities 5 and 9 while acquiring the two locks, and returned through 5 to 2 as they were released.

Every exception entry and selected return PC matched the reconstructed frames. The QMP journal supplied frame addresses and priority, wait and ownership observations. Task control flow distinguished successful acquire from timeout. The raw QEMU exception trace supplies PCs and exception types. [Independent verdict, UART, journal and trace](evd-018.json)

Seventy Rust host tests passed, including an independent donor oracle over 3,456 configurations. Eight Python response-analysis tests and eight serialized guest regressions also passed. The mutex build emitted an unused-method warning for the shared ready queue. [Integration record](evd-018-regressions.json)

Verified 2026-11-24 with Rust 1.95.0 and QEMU 6.2.0 virt, GICv2 and 128 MiB RAM. ELF SHA-256 `6ee5cbfa1b706c3ef689e7a6ef3de5b6b00cfb79aaf2cc3945764a87ffd3e462`. Physical maximum blocking under interference and product limits remain open. CHG-020 · POL-014 · IMP-018 · VER-002/005/023.
